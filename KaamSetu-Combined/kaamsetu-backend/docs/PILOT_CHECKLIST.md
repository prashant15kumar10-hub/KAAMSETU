# KAAMSETU pilot checklist

Do not begin a real-worker pilot until privacy/consent wording, emergency responsibilities, support escalation, and deployment security have been reviewed by the responsible operator.

## Before the pilot

- [ ] Select a small geography and a narrow set of job categories; recruit workers and contractors voluntarily.
- [ ] Explain that the product is a pilot and clarify which workflows are live versus manual/unavailable.
- [ ] Confirm worker access does not require personal smartphone ownership or UPI use.
- [ ] Review Hindi translations with a fluent native speaker; test with low-literacy users.
- [ ] Confirm job terms, cancellation rules, wage reporting, payment acknowledgements and dispute escalation are understandable.
- [ ] Train contractors/facilitators not to change agreed terms or impersonate worker confirmation.
- [ ] Publish only locally verified support contacts with source, eligibility notes and verification date. Until verified, do not invent a help directory.
- [ ] For any family relay handled outside the app, obtain consent and document the real operating procedure; do not claim automated delivery.
- [ ] Verify HTTPS, DNS, firewall, secrets, admin MFA recovery, logs, backups and an off-site restore drill on the target host.
- [ ] Arrange independent security testing appropriate to the risks before handling sensitive data at scale.

## During the pilot — collect minimum necessary data

- [ ] Jobs posted, applications, confirmed bookings and completed jobs.
- [ ] Wage records reconciled, reported/acknowledged/disputed payments and advances/disputes tracked outside the app if no ledger exists.
- [ ] Contractor coordination time and repeat usage.
- [ ] Tasks completed independently versus with facilitator assistance.
- [ ] Failed contact attempts and message delivery outcomes only if an actual relay process is used.
- [ ] Worker retention, willingness to pay and operating cost per completed worker-shift.
- [ ] Safety incidents and complaints using a reviewed, access-restricted process.

## After the pilot

- [ ] Report results with sample size, period, limitations and missing data.
- [ ] Separate observed measurements from estimates and opinions.
- [ ] Review worker/contractor feedback and adverse outcomes before changing ranking or allocation policy.
- [ ] Do not infer that simulated tests establish usability or that a small pilot establishes national demand.
- [ ] Decide whether to expand, revise or stop based on worker safety, wage transparency, actual utility and cost.
